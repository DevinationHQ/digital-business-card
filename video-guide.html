Use one short, hyperrealistic work scene as your Digital Business Card hero. Start with a clear image, keep the motion simple and retain a still fallback.

1. Choose the reference

Use a permitted image with a clear face and a believable work setting. Keep the person and action central so the video works in a tighter card crop. For these examples, use the matching Theo or Mira work reference.

Download Theo’s work reference · Download Mira’s work reference

2. Generate in Gemini

Open Gemini’s video-creation option and attach the reference. Paste the matching prompt below. Request portrait 9:16 and a single roughly 8-second shot where those controls are available. The options depend on your model, account and region.

The desired format is a premium film-like shot with subtle motion, not a fast montage. Identity, fingers, object geometry and a seamless loop still need checking after generation.

Theo — creative technologist & 3D designer

Create a hyperrealistic, premium live-action hero video using the attached image as the exact opening frame and identity reference. Keep Theo’s face, hair, beard, skin tone and clothing consistent throughout. He is a creative technologist and 3D designer working in the architectural-visualisation studio shown.

One continuous 8-second shot, portrait 9:16 if available. The camera makes a very slow, smooth 15-centimetre sideways dolly with a slight push-in, creating subtle parallax between the architectural model, Theo and the studio. Theo makes one small, natural mouse movement, studies the design, then shifts his gaze slightly toward the model with a restrained, satisfied expression. His face stays clearly visible. The monitor display faces Theo. The camera sees its plain dark back and side edge, as in the reference. Preserve this orientation throughout: do not turn the display toward the viewer or reveal an architectural picture on its back.

Near-black navy environment, soft cool monitor light, restrained warm amber practical light, natural skin texture, realistic optics and shallow depth of field. Premium architecture-film cinematography. Calm motion with a short settled opening and ending, suitable for a looping website hero. Preserve the room geometry, model, hands and clothing. No cuts, morphing, extra fingers, floating objects, holograms, exaggerated gestures, talking, lip-sync, added captions, added logos or soundtrack. Keep essential action central so the video survives a tighter card crop.

Mira — ceramic designer

Create a hyperrealistic, premium craft-film hero video using the attached image as the exact opening frame and identity reference. Keep Mira’s face, wavy brown hair, skin tone, earrings, cream blouse and terracotta apron consistent throughout. She is the ceramic designer working at the pottery wheel shown.

One continuous 8-second shot, portrait 9:16 if available. Make a very slow, smooth 15-centimetre camera glide with a gentle push-in. The wheel rotates steadily while Mira keeps both hands gently against the same simple shallow bowl. Show only subtle, believable finger pressure; the bowl retains its existing size and shape. She glances naturally from the rim toward the bowl’s centre and gives a barely perceptible smile. Keep her face and the making action in the same composition, rather than cutting between them.

Warm afternoon window light, realistic wet-clay highlights, tactile linen, natural skin detail and softly blurred studio shelves. Premium independent-design documentary cinematography. A short settled opening and ending make it suitable for a looping website hero. No cuts, sudden zooms, changing pottery, hand distortion, extra fingers, floating tools, talking, lip-sync, added captions, added logos or soundtrack. Keep her face and bowl central for a tighter card crop.

3. Review and download

Watch the full shot and a few repeats. Check the face, hands, props and any unwanted writing. If the scene drifts, ask for less movement. Download the generated file; preserve any provider-required labels or watermark. Do not present synthetic work footage as a real completed client project.

Keep the original export, then prepare a small delivery copy for your website. Theo and Mira’s generated videos are already included in their example profiles.

4. Add your own video

In your own GitHub repository, open assets/video, choose Add file → Upload files, upload my-hero.mp4, then Commit changes to main. Repeat inside assets/photos with a fallback image named my-photo.jpg. Keep each browser upload below 25 MB. Wait for the next successful Actions deployment.

Open Make yours on your own published website. Set Portrait file path to assets/photos/my-photo.jpg and Hero video file path to assets/video/my-hero.mp4. Choose muted automatic play or visitor-initiated play. Preview, download JSON, and replace the full contents of data/card.json in your repository. Commit to main, wait for deployment and refresh the main website.

Entering a path does not upload a file. Devination’s demo cannot preview media uploaded only to your separate copy. Follow the full beginner setup guide to create and publish your copy first.

A short H.264 MP4 is a practical starting point. Keep a high-quality master separately and aim for a small delivery copy, roughly 1–3 MB if quality allows. Use the real exported codec and file size as your guide.

5. Check playback and the fallback

Test on a phone. Check the crop, loading time and pause button. Reduced-motion preferences prevent autoplay. If a browser blocks autoplay, the still image stays available and the visitor can press Play. The clip is muted and pauses when offscreen or when the page is hidden.
